# GEX Dashboard — Scope & Direction

**Status:** Decision document, locked after pressure-testing across multiple sessions.
**Owner:** Adam, Product & Analytics, Purpose Investments
**Last revised:** 2026-05-06

---

## 1. What this tool is

A descriptive context tool. Given today's CBOE option positioning across a defined index universe, it computes:

- **GEX** — net dealer gamma exposure, expressed as dollars of delta-hedging flow per 1% spot move
- **VEX** — net dealer vanna exposure, dollars of hedging flow per 1% IV move
- **CHEX** — net dealer charm exposure, dollars of hedging flow per calendar day

These numbers describe the structural dealer positioning that conditions intraday and short-horizon market behavior — vol suppression vs amplification, mean reversion vs trend, post-event squeezes, OPEX dynamics.

**What it is not:** a predictive signal, a trading edge, or a backtested strategy. The framework is descriptive context for the PM desk to layer alongside their other inputs. Dealer-flow dynamics have weak academic predictive content beyond short-horizon realized vol; this tool does not claim to overcome that.

---

## 2. Universe — locked at three tickers

**SPY, QQQ, IWM only.**

### Why these three

For the dealer-flow framework to be interpretively valid, three conditions must hold:

1. **Institutional hedging flow dominates** — required for the standard "dealers long calls, short puts" positioning convention to be defensible.
2. **Options chain liquidity sufficient that OI reflects real positioning** — required for the math to be signal rather than noise.
3. **Underlying broad enough that idiosyncratic single-name flow doesn't dominate aggregate** — required for regime classification to be about market-level dynamics rather than name-specific quirks.

SPY, QQQ, IWM meet all three. Almost no other commonly-tracked ticker does.

### What was deliberately dropped

| Tier | Examples | Why dropped |
|------|----------|-------------|
| Sector ETFs | XLF, XLE, XLK | Mixed flow, convention shakier, marginal interpretive value |
| Rates / Commodities | TLT, GLD | Theoretical premise (equity hedging dynamics) does not apply |
| Single names | NVDA, TSLA, AAPL | Convention varies by name; out of scope without name-by-name positioning research |
| Purpose Yield Shares | PHY, PYF, others | Positioning systematically inverted by design — see Section 3 |

### Trade-off accepted

Dropping the broader ticker tier means the original $200M neutral-band floor (calibrated against illiquid tickers like XLF/EFA/EWJ/TLT) is no longer empirically anchored. The threshold needs recalibration against SPY/QQQ/IWM noise levels — see Section 6.

## 3. Why Purpose Yield Shares are not on the dashboard

These are Purpose's own products and the desk should expect a clear answer to "why aren't they here?"

**The dealer-flow framework assumes the standard institutional positioning convention: dealers are long calls and short puts.** This convention was empirically derived from SPX/SPY institutional hedging flow and underpins the sign of every GEX, VEX, and CHEX number the tool produces.

Covered call funds invert this convention by design. The fund itself is the systematic call writer, which means dealers on the other side of those calls are *short calls*, not long them. Applying the standard sign convention produces numbers that are mechanically wrong for these tickers — not "uncertain," actually backward in expectation.

**Three options were considered:**

1. Drop entirely from dashboard — *committed.*
2. Keep as raw-data cards without GEX/regime — rejected; raw OI data without interpretive context adds little.
3. Keep with "POSITIONING INVERTED" badge — rejected; a regime label next to a badge gets read with the same confidence as the SPY label and produces misleading impressions.

**Decision:** Drop entirely. The framework cannot honestly be applied to these tickers, so the dashboard does not pretend to. The desk gets cleaner positioning context from the existing fund-level reporting workflows.

## 4. Methodology

### Data source

CBOE delayed quotes CDN (`cdn.cboe.com/api/global/delayed_quotes/options/{ticker}.json`). Free, no auth, 15-minute delay. Pulls spot, IV30, full options chain with CBOE-computed Greeks (American model — accounts for early exercise and dividends, materially better than Black-Scholes for US equity options).

### Computation

- **Gamma, delta, vega, theta** — directly from CBOE (American model)
- **Vanna, charm** — computed in-tool via Black-Scholes (European approximation; CBOE does not publish these). Directionally correct, not precise for deep ITM or near-expiry where early exercise matters
- **Sign convention** — `+1 for calls, -1 for puts` (standard institutional positioning assumption, valid for the index universe)

### Filters

| Filter | Value | Rationale |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| Min OI | 100 | Drops illiquid strikes — garbage OI is noise |
| Min DTE | **1, locked** | See note below |
| Max IV | 3.0 (300%) | Drops stale or clearly broken quotes |

**Why min_dte=1 and not 0:** Three Greek-derived columns drive the dashboard (GEX, VEX, CHEX). At T → 0, charm and Black-Scholes gamma are mathematically singular at ATM strikes; vanna approaches zero cleanly. Including 0DTE would require invented `T_min` floors for charm and the gamma profile sweep but not for vanna — producing inconsistent treatment across the three columns where two are stabilized by arbitrary conventions and one isn't. Excluding 0DTE keeps the math internally consistent across all three columns. The cost is that absolute Net GEX runs ~25–30% smaller than commercial sources that do include 0DTE; this is documented in the caveats and validation sections.

Commercial sources that include 0DTE (e.g., InsiderFinance) do so via opaque singularity-handling conventions. Adopting those conventions to match their absolute magnitudes would amount to curve-fitting to their output rather than producing an independently-implemented number.

### Derived levels

- **Zero-gamma level (ZGL)** — spot at which net GEX crosses zero, computed via linear interpolation across a ±15% spot grid
- **Call wall / put wall** — strikes with largest positive / most negative aggregate GEX

## 5. Validation status

### Question A: Is the implementation mathematically correct?

**Status: closed.**

#### Pass/fail criteria

Two structural levels and one absolute-magnitude check were locked before running:
- ZGL within 1.5% of spot — **PASS** across SPY (0.6 pts), QQQ (0.43%), IWM (0.40%)
- Put wall within one strike interval — **PASS** exact match across all three tickers
- Net Gamma within 15% of external source — **closed without verdict**

#### Why the absolute-magnitude criterion was closed

Commercial sources publish Net GEX values up to ~30% apart on the same ticker on the same day due to methodology differences in expiry inclusion:
- Barchart: 4 nearby expirations, per-1% scaling (published)
- InsiderFinance: presumably full chain incl. 0DTE (not published; inferred from absolute magnitudes)
- This tool: ≥1 DTE, all expiries up to ~3 years

No canonical reference exists. The 15%-relative criterion presumed a single ground truth that does not exist in the ecosystem. Recognizing the criterion was structurally flawed only after running the comparison is honest; the fix is not "add an absolute floor" (that would be rationalizing IWM's failure) but "drop the criterion and replace with methodology disclosure" (a first-principles correction valid regardless of which ticker passed or failed).

#### What is validated

ZGL and put walls (the structural levels driving regime context). Independent implementation hitting the same sign-change crossing within 0.6% across three OI distributions is strong evidence the gamma engine is correct.

#### What is approximate

Net GEX absolute magnitude. Use sign and order of magnitude; treat the precise number as approximate.

### Question B: Does the tool produce signals a PM can trade against?

**Status: deliberately not pursued.** Pursuing this requires either an expensive backtest with no clear null hypothesis, or an observation period to generate a specific question worth testing. The 60-day observation log (Section 6) is the right intermediate step. A formal predictive-content study is premature until there's behavior worth testing.

## 7. Caveats that ship with the dashboard

These are the honest interpretive limits of the tool. They live on the dashboard, not in this doc, so a PM reading the regime label sees them at the same time as the number.

1. **Positioning convention.** All numbers assume dealers are long calls and short puts. Valid for the index universe; not valid for covered call writers, retail-skewed single names, or non-equity products. (Why those tickers aren't on the dashboard.)
2. **OI staleness.** OI is from the prior session's clearing. On gap-open days or post-event repositioning days, today's GEX uses yesterday's book — meaningful when overnight repositioning is large.
3. **0DTE excluded.** Dashboard shows ≥1 DTE only. SPX 0DTE is now ~40–55% of daily option volume; the tool understates intraday-flow gamma by design.
4. **Vanna and charm are European approximations.** Directionally useful, less precise near expiry or deep ITM.
5. **Descriptive, not predictive.** The tool describes structural dealer positioning. It is not a trading signal or alpha source.
6. **Net GEX absolute magnitude is methodology-dependent.** Commercial dealer-flow tools publish values up to ~30% apart on the same ticker due to differences in expiry inclusion (Barchart: 4 nearby expiries; InsiderFinance: full chain incl. 0DTE; this tool: ≥1 DTE). No canonical reference exists. Use sign and order of magnitude; treat the precise number as approximate. ZGL, put wall, and call wall are robust across these methodology differences and are the load-bearing outputs.
